THE PRINCIPLE THIS WORKSHEET PROTECTS AGAINST
Underpricing is more dangerous than overpricing — because underpricing is silent.
If you price too high, someone will tell you. They'll hesitate, push back, or walk away. You'll know to adjust. If you price too low, no one says anything. Clients sign on, work begins, and you're stuck at a number that doesn't fund your business. From then on, every price conversation starts from that floor. This worksheet is built to keep you from defaulting to the safe-feeling number.

STEP THREE · FRAMING & FUTURE INCREASE

Two pieces of language. The early-adopter framing, and the future-increase trigger.

The lesson's argument: your first price isn't low because you're worth less. It's a deliberate founding-client offer. The framing matters as much as the number, and the future increase needs to be named at the start — not surprising clients twelve months later.

PIECE 01 · EARLY-ADOPTER POSITIONING
The one sentence that frames the founding-client offer.

The lesson is direct: "Introductory rate while we build our clinical reference base" sounds completely different from "we're still figuring out what to charge." One is deliberate. The other is uncertain. Write your version.

The lesson's example: 20% off the defensible price. Standard range: 15–25%. Below 10% isn't enough to feel like a deliberate offer; above 30% structurally undermines the price.

"Founding clinics" / "Founding members" / "Charter clients" / "Early-access cohort"

What you're building, why the founding cohort matters, what they get in return. The deliberate framing.

"Introductory rate for founding clinics while we build our clinical outcomes documentation — a deliberate choice to establish reference relationships in exchange for feedback and referrals."

PIECE 02 · THE FUTURE-INCREASE TRIGGER
Build the price increase into the first conversation.

The lesson is direct: founders who skip this come back twelve months later with thirty clients at a price they can't raise. Name the trigger and timeframe at the start. The increase is expected, not surprising.

Specific and observable. Not "when we feel ready." Examples: "first clinical outcomes documentation complete" / "fifty paying clients reached" / "v2 launch shipped."

When you expect the trigger to fire. Honest estimate, not aspirational.

The lesson's example: "Clinics that come on board now lock in the founding rate for their first year." A specific protection that turns the future increase into an incentive to act now.

"Clinics that come on board now lock in the founding rate for their first twelve months, regardless of when the price increase takes effect."
